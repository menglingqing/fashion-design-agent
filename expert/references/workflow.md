## 1. Opportunity framing

**Purpose:** Convert market signals and brand goals into a bounded design opportunity.

**Inputs:** channel/market, period, category data, search/social signals, brand position, consumer feedback, past product performance.

**Method:**

1. Record source, date range, geography, channel, metric definition, and sample limitations.
2. Separate mature-volume categories, high-growth opportunities, image-building categories, and experimental bets.
3. Map customer pain points by scene, climate, movement, styling, care, and price.
4. State what the brand can credibly win, not only what is trending.

**Output:** opportunity matrix with evidence, confidence, brand fit, commercial potential, and design implication.

**Gate:** Confirm target market, season, commercial objective, and opportunity priorities.

## 2. Category and SKU architecture

**Purpose:** Translate strategy into a balanced product range.

**Inputs:** revenue or sales target, planned price, category mix, gender/unisex mix, launch waves, channel needs, carryover products.

**Method:**

1. Classify each slot as traffic, volume, profit, image, continuity, or experiment.
2. Calculate only with supplied numbers. Typical formulas:
   - Planned sales units = planned GSV / planned selling price.
   - Style efficiency = planned GSV / SPU count.
   - Mix percentage = category GSV / total GSV.
3. Separate new development, carryover, color extension, and ODM/OEM.
4. Check range balance across category, gender, price, scene, color, material, and launch wave.

**Output:** category/SKU plan with role, SPU, price, target, launch, and owner.

**Gate:** Confirm total target, SPU envelope, price architecture, and key bets.

## 3. Story and scenario definition

**Purpose:** Give the range a coherent consumer-facing reason to exist.

**Inputs:** brand codes, seasonal context, cultural/social signals, target scenes, climate, categories.

**Method:**

1. Write one tension: what the wearer wants versus what current products fail to provide.
2. Create one collection proposition, three to five keywords, and a visual/material language.
3. Map each scene to wearer, action, weather, emotional need, product need, and launch wave.
4. Assign categories to scenes; reject mood-board images that do not influence product decisions.

**Output:** story statement, mood direction, scenario map, and category-to-scene map.

**Gate:** Confirm that every major category supports the story and a real wearing scene.

## 4. Competitor and bestseller analysis

**Purpose:** Identify repeatable market patterns and white-space opportunities without copying.

**Inputs:** comparable products, verified product pages/data, user reviews, prices, launch dates, materials, details, visuals.

**Method:**

1. Separate business benchmark, design benchmark, material/function benchmark, and communication benchmark.
2. Compare consistent fields: brand, price, launch, silhouette, construction, material, function, color, reviews, marketing resources, and evidence quality.
3. Extract patterns, not isolated anecdotes.
4. Read negative reviews as design opportunities.
5. Build `retain / transform / avoid` rules.

**Output:** competitor matrix, bestseller DNA, pain-point list, and differentiation opportunities.

**Gate:** Confirm the target price/position and the dimensions of differentiation.

## 5. Product slot briefs

**Purpose:** Turn range slots into designable problems.

**Inputs:** SKU role, target customer, scene, price, competitor patterns, materials, brand story.

**Method:** Define job-to-be-done, silhouette, fit, key details, material/function requirements, colors, cost assumptions, must-haves, exclusions, and success measures.

**Output:** one brief per SPU or reusable link.

**Gate:** Confirm the non-negotiables before generating detailed directions.

## 6. Materials, functions, colors, and trims

**Purpose:** Align sensory quality, performance, aesthetics, cost, and lead time.

**Inputs:** category needs, season/climate, supplier library, test reports, target cost, MOQ, lead time, color standards.

**Method:**

1. Define required wearer benefit before naming a technology.
2. Separate must-have performance, preferred hand-feel, visual effect, care, sustainability, and fallback.
3. For every performance claim, record evidence and test standard/status.
4. Build color roles: core commercial, seasonal anchor, accent, and carryover.
5. Check color application by material because sheen and dye behavior change perception.

**Output:** material brief, function-evidence matrix, trim plan, color palette, and colorway allocation.

**Gate:** Confirm available materials, test evidence, target cost, MOQ, lead time, and lab-dip/strike-off status.

## 7. Design development and selection

**Purpose:** Generate distinct, viable concepts and select one transparently.

**Inputs:** approved brief, reference DNA, material/color direction, brand codes, constraints.

**Method:**

1. Create a divergence matrix across silhouette, proportion, construction, closure, collar/hood, sleeve, pocket, material blocking, color, and trim.
2. Generate three to ten directions with meaningful differences.
3. Score each direction against brand fit, consumer benefit, differentiation, commercial potential, production feasibility, cost risk, and reference similarity.
4. Recommend one lead and one backup; explain tradeoffs.

**Output:** direction cards, comparison matrix, selected concept, and revision notes.

**Gate:** Select concept and freeze the visible design language before detailed handoff.

## 8. Technical handoff

**Purpose:** Convert intent into an unambiguous development package.

**Inputs:** selected design, blocks, measurements, materials, trims, construction standards, labels, artwork.

**Method:**

1. Produce front/back technical flats and side/detail views when needed.
2. Number callouts consistently across views and specifications.
3. Define POM measurements, grade rules if available, BOM, stitch/seam/finish, artwork placement, colorways, labels, packaging, and test requirements.
4. Mark unknowns as TBD with owner and due date.

**Output:** tech-pack content or company-format development brief.

**Gate:** Cross-check drawings, BOM, specs, colorways, and construction notes before supplier release.

## 9. Sample review and change control

**Purpose:** Evaluate the physical sample against intended fit, function, appearance, cost, and timeline.

**Inputs:** sample photos/measurements, fit model data, comments, test results, costing, prior version.

**Review order:**

1. Safety and critical function
2. Fit, balance, and mobility
3. Silhouette and proportion
4. Construction and durability
5. Material, color, and hand-feel
6. Details and workmanship
7. Cost, lead time, and scalability

**Output:** issue log containing location, evidence, severity, requested change, measurement or visual target, owner, due date, and verification method.

**Gate:** Approve, approve with corrections, resample, or reject. Never use vague comments such as “再高级一点” without observable criteria.

## 10. Launch and iteration

**Purpose:** Preserve design truth through selling communication and learn from results.

**Inputs:** final approved sample, verified features/tests, target scene, product imagery, launch calendar, sales/review/return data.

**Method:**

1. Create claim-evidence-benefit copy.
2. Align product name, story, selling points, imagery, and actual garment.
3. Track launch wave, channel, owner, asset readiness, and stock readiness.
4. After launch, compare plan versus actual by style role, price, color, size, returns, and reviews.
5. Classify next action: repeat, recolor, adjust, reprice, reposition, or discontinue.

**Output:** launch handoff, content brief, and post-launch review.

**Gate:** Feed validated learning into the next seasonal opportunity frame.
